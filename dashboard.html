Query 1 · Trend
Victoria's mortality trend reveals a post-pandemic rebound, not a pandemic peak

Deaths in Victoria held steady through the 2020–2021 lockdown years, then jumped by 5,492 in 2022 — the sharpest year-on-year rise in the dataset.

Finding. Deaths remained relatively stable in 2020–2021 despite the pandemic, likely because strict lockdowns suppressed other causes of death (road accidents, influenza). The sharp 2022 spike (+13.4% vs 2021) reflects the COVID-19 rebound after restrictions lifted, compounded by healthcare deferred during the pandemic years.
SELECT year, deaths
FROM mortality_summary
WHERE state = 'Victoria' AND sex = 'Persons'
ORDER BY year ASC;
Query 2 · Ranking
Crude death rate by state, 2023

Tasmania records the highest raw death rate in the country — but crude rates mix in age structure, so this ranking answers "how many died" per capita, not "how healthy is this state."

Finding. Tasmania leads at 885.1 deaths per 100,000 — driven by its older population structure — while the Northern Territory ranks last (496.2) despite having the country's worst health outcomes on other measures, because it has a much younger population. Crude rates alone are insufficient for comparing regions; they reflect age structure as much as health system performance.
SELECT state, crude_rate_per_100000
FROM mortality_summary
WHERE year = 2023 AND sex = 'Persons'
ORDER BY crude_rate_per_100000 DESC;
Query 3 · Health equity
Average median age at death by state, 2019–2023

The starkest equity signal in the dataset: Territorians are, on average, dying 15 years younger than South Australians.

Finding. The Northern Territory has the lowest median age at death (67.2 years) — a full 15 years below South Australia (82.8). This is the clearest indicator of health inequity in the dataset, consistent with limited healthcare access and higher chronic disease burden in remote and Indigenous communities across the NT.
SELECT state, AVG(median_age) AS avg_median_age_at_death
FROM mortality_summary
WHERE sex = 'Persons'
GROUP BY state
ORDER BY avg_median_age_at_death ASC;
Query 4 & 5 · Absolute vs. population-adjusted
Premature deaths, 2019–2023: raw counts mislead, adjusted rates don't

Premature death = death before age 75. Switch views to see why NSW looks like the biggest problem in absolute terms, but the Northern Territory is where the burden is actually heaviest.

Finding (absolute). NSW (90,745) and Victoria (68,135) lead in absolute premature deaths — but this largely just reflects their much larger populations. Absolute counts alone don't identify where health intervention is most needed.
-- Query 4: absolute total
SELECT state, SUM(premature_deaths) AS total_premature_deaths
FROM mortality_summary
WHERE sex = 'Persons'
GROUP BY state
ORDER BY total_premature_deaths DESC;

-- Query 5: population-adjusted rate
SELECT state,
       AVG((premature_deaths * 1.0 / population) * 100000) AS premature_death_rate_per_100k
FROM mortality_summary
WHERE sex = 'Persons'
GROUP BY state
ORDER BY premature_death_rate_per_100k DESC;
Query 6 · Join
Leading cause of death by state

Joining mortality summary with leading causes shows coronary heart disease dominates nationally — except where an older population profile pushes dementia to the top.

Coronary heart disease leads
Dementia / Alzheimer's leads (exception)
Finding. Coronary heart disease is the leading cause of death in 6 of 8 states and territories. South Australia and the ACT are the exceptions — both show Dementia/Alzheimer's disease ranking first, consistent with their older population profiles where people survive long enough to develop degenerative conditions. The Northern Territory's #2 and #3 causes (diabetes, lung cancer) diverge from the national pattern (dementia, cerebrovascular disease) entirely — another marker of a distinct health profile.
SELECT m.state,
       AVG((m.premature_deaths * 1.0 / m.population) * 100000) AS premature_death_rate_per_100k,
       l.cause_of_death AS leading_cause_of_death
FROM mortality_summary m
JOIN leading_causes l ON m.state = l.state
WHERE m.sex = 'Persons' AND l.sex = 'Persons' AND l.rank = 1
GROUP BY m.state, l.cause_of_death
ORDER BY premature_death_rate_per_100k DESC;
